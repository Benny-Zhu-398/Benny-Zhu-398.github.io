Research · CMU Search-Based Planning Lab · Advisor: Prof. Maxim Likhachev

A MuJoCo-native planner that searches over physics rollouts and reuses a library of past pushes to cut search cost on related problems — contact-frame-aware replay raises execution success from 2% to 90%.

Team entry · Industrial Task Board Assembly track, 2nd RoCo Challenge @ IROS 2026 · in progress

A bimanual pipeline that picks nine industrial parts and assembles them onto a task board in Isaac Sim, trained via disturbance-augmented imitation learning per part group.

Flow Matching for Locomotion

24-782 · Team Stitch, with Gopalakrishnan Thirunellai Venkitachalam

A symmetry-regularized PPO expert feeds a Transformer Flow Matching model that generalizes continuously from 0.5–3.0 m/s, cutting gait asymmetry 83% versus plain PPO.

Dexterous In-Hand Manipulation with Shadow Hand

With Xinxuan Tang · Mechanical Engineering, CMU

A comparison of PPO, SAC, and CEM on Shadow Hand cube reorientation, plus domain randomization and a diffusion-based GenPO policy — large-scale parallel PPO wins outright, and GenPO matches it with smoother, lower-variance training.

Optimal Control of Quadrotor for Obstacle Avoidance

24-785 · Team 8, with Burhan Shirose, Qihang Shan, Madeline Hoedemaker

Three planar quadrotors navigate two moving obstacles via nonconvex trajectory optimization, cross-validated with Julia's fmincon and SciPy's SLSQP — fmincon converged 7× faster with lower tracking error.

Proprioceptive Navigation Through Vegetation

16-350 · with David Seong, Weichen Qiu · Mar – May 2025

A Husky robot in Gazebo/ROS2 treats vegetation as traversable-at-a-cost rather than an obstacle, updating a Bayesian cost map from proprioceptive feedback so D* Lite replans a cheaper path than static A*.

Tilted: A Low-Cost Mechatronic Maze-Solving Platform

24-668 Mechatronics Applications in Mechanical Engineering · with Avery Kress, Nathan O’Neill, Sander Schulman

A 2-DOF tilting-plate robot solves arbitrary laser-cut mazes with vision, A*, and a discrete LQR — 100% success on straight and single-turn mazes, dropping to 60–70% on tighter multi-turn paths.
